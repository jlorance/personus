# Profile Page Design Patterns Across Platform Categories

**Research Date:** 2026-02-14
**Purpose:** Inform Personus.ai persona page design by analyzing layout patterns, visual hierarchy, trust signals, and eye-attention "bullseye" zones across 20+ platforms in 5 categories.

---

## Eye-Tracking Foundations

Before diving into specific platforms, it helps to understand how eyes actually move across profile pages.

**F-Pattern** (text-heavy profiles): Users scan horizontally across the top, then drop down the left side and scan horizontally again, forming an "F." This dominates on LinkedIn, Upwork, and other text-dense professional profiles. The implication: the top-left quadrant is the highest-value real estate. Names, titles, and key stats belong there.

**Z-Pattern** (visual/sparse profiles): On pages with less text and more imagery, eyes move in a Z -- top-left to top-right, diagonal to bottom-left, then across to bottom-right. This applies to Instagram, Dribbble, Linktree, and portfolio hero sections. The implication: the top-right is the CTA hotspot; bottom-right is the secondary action zone.

**Gutenberg Diagram** (balanced layouts): For evenly distributed content, attention flows from the "Primary Optical Area" (top-left) to the "Terminal Area" (bottom-right), with "strong fallow" (top-right) and "weak fallow" (bottom-left) zones. Profile cards and grid layouts often follow this.

**Key universal finding:** The top 300-400px of any profile page receives 80%+ of initial attention. Everything below the fold competes for scrolling commitment.

## 1. Professional Profiles

### LinkedIn

**Hero Zone (top ~400px):**

- Banner image (1584x396px) spanning full width -- sets the emotional/brand tone
- Profile photo (circular, overlapping the banner bottom-left) -- the single strongest attention anchor
- Name in large bold type directly right of photo
- Headline (120 chars) immediately below name -- this is the #1 text people read
- Location + connection count + mutual connections
- "Open to" banner (green) when active
- Row of CTAs: Connect, Message, More

**Primary CTA:** "Connect" (blue button, top-right of the hero card)

**Trust Signals:**

- Mutual connections ("12 mutual connections") -- social proof via network overlap
- "500+" connections badge
- Blue verification badge (new in 2024-25)
- "Open to Work" / "Hiring" status banners
- Profile completeness is implicit (empty sections look bad)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Skills section with endorsement counts lives mid-page (requires scroll)
- Recommendations section is near bottom -- full-text testimonials
- "Featured" section sits just below About -- allows pinning proof (articles, media, links)
- Profiles with a completed Featured section get ~30% more views

**Visual Treatment:** Dense but structured. Card-based sections on white/light background. Formal, corporate aesthetic. Information-heavy. Very little whitespace compared to personal profiles. Mobile-first (70%+ users browse on mobile).

**Unique pattern:** The "About" section shows only 2-3 lines before "see more" -- the opening sentence is critical, functioning like a hook.

### Upwork

**Hero Zone:**

- Profile photo (large, left-aligned)
- Name + title (e.g., "Full-Stack Developer")
- Hourly rate prominently displayed
- Job Success Score (percentage badge, e.g., "98% Job Success")
- Availability status ("Available now" green indicator)
- Total earnings / hours worked
- Location + timezone

**Primary CTA:** "Invite to Job" or "Hire" button

**Trust Signals:**

- Job Success Score (the dominant metric -- large, green, percentage-based)
- "Top Rated" / "Top Rated Plus" / "Rising Talent" badges
- Total earnings displayed (social proof via money earned)
- Hours worked on platform
- Skill-specific certifications

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Client reviews with star ratings appear mid-page, attached to specific completed projects
- Each review shows project title, budget, duration, and client feedback
- Reviews are chronological, most recent first

**Visual Treatment:** Utilitarian, dense, data-forward. White background, structured cards. The profile feels like a resume + report card hybrid. Skills displayed as tags/pills. Portfolio section shows project thumbnails in a grid.

### AngelList / Wellfound

**Hero Zone:**

- Profile photo + name + current role/company
- One-line tagline ("Building the future of X")
- Location + years of experience
- Looking for: role types, company sizes, markets
- Skills as horizontal tag pills

**Primary CTA:** "Connect" or "Request Intro"

**Trust Signals:**

- Current company affiliation (with logo)
- Investment history (for investors)
- Founded companies list
- Shared connections in the startup ecosystem

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** Minimal -- the platform relies on network graph (who you know) and company affiliation rather than explicit endorsements. The company you work for IS the trust signal.

**Visual Treatment:** Clean, minimal, startup-aesthetic. Lots of whitespace. Dark text on white. The profile is secondary to the job listings -- it's a means to an end, not a destination.

---

### Fiverr

**Hero Zone:**

- Seller level badge (Level 1 / Level 2 / Top Rated / Pro -- prominently displayed)
- Profile photo + display name
- One-line description
- Location + member since + avg response time + last delivery
- Star rating + number of reviews
- Languages spoken

**Primary CTA:** Links to specific "Gigs" (service listings) rather than a single profile CTA

**Trust Signals:**

- Seller level badges (tiered system, visually prominent)
- "Pro" verified badge (vetted by Fiverr)
- Star rating with review count
- Response time and delivery metrics
- "Seller Plus" members can hand-pick reviews to highlight in a "What people loved" section

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Reviews are attached to individual Gigs, not the overall profile
- Highlighted/pinned reviews appear at Gig level
- Aggregate rating visible on profile

**Visual Treatment:** Commercial, transactional. White background, product-card aesthetic. The profile is a storefront -- each Gig is a product card with thumbnail, title, price, and rating. Dense information, optimized for conversion.

---

### Behance

**Hero Zone:**

- Banner image (full width)
- Circular profile photo (overlapping banner, left)
- Name + location + creative field tags
- Follower/following/appreciations counts
- "Follow" and "Hire Me" buttons
- Short bio text

**Primary CTA:** "Follow" (for discovery) or "Hire Me" (for work)

**Trust Signals:**

- Appreciation counts on projects (like counts, but for creative work)
- Featured by Behance / Adobe curators
- Project view counts
- Adobe portfolio integration

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** No formal endorsement system. Trust is built through project engagement (views, appreciations, comments). The work IS the endorsement.

**Visual Treatment:** Visual-first, gallery-dominant. Below the hero, the page becomes a grid of project thumbnails (808x632px). Minimal text. The profile is a gallery with a header. White/light background, generous spacing between project cards.

---

### Dribbble

**Hero Zone:**

- Profile photo (circular, centered or left)
- Name + tagline
- Location + "Available for work" status
- Follower/following counts
- "Hire Me" and "Follow" buttons
- Bio text (brief)

**Primary CTA:** "Hire Me" button (for Pro members)

**Trust Signals:**

- "Pro" member badge
- Follower count (social proof)
- "Available for work" status
- Shot likes/saves/views

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** No endorsement system. Social proof comes from shot engagement metrics (likes, saves, views) and follower count. Being featured in Dribbble's curated collections is the ultimate trust signal.

**Visual Treatment:** The most visual-first of all professional platforms. Below the hero, it's a masonry grid of "shots" (400x300px thumbnails). Hover reveals title, likes, saves. Extremely airy -- the work dominates, profile info is minimal. Pink/brand-color accents. Feels like an art gallery.

## 2. Personal Profiles

### Instagram

**Hero Zone (mobile-first, ~350px):**

- Profile photo (circular, top-left, ~77px on mobile)
- Username + verified badge
- Posts / Followers / Following count (horizontal row, top-right)
- Display name (bold)
- Category label ("Digital Creator", "Musician")
- Bio text (150 chars max)
- Link (single URL or Linktree)
- Story Highlights row (circular thumbnails with labels)

**Primary CTA:** "Follow" button (blue, prominent) + "Message"

**Trust Signals:**

- Blue verification badge
- Follower count (the dominant metric)
- Category label (professional account type)
- Story Highlights (show consistent activity/themes)
- Follower-to-following ratio (implicit)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** No formal endorsements. Trust is built through follower count, engagement on posts, and being tagged/mentioned by others. "Mutual followers" serves as network-based trust.

**Visual Treatment:** Extremely compact hero, then a grid of square (now 3:4 ratio) post thumbnails. The profile IS the grid. Personality is expressed through visual consistency of the grid, bio copywriting, and story highlight covers. Mobile-native. Dark mode available. No cards -- flat, edge-to-edge content.

---

### TikTok

**Hero Zone:**

- Profile photo (circular, centered)
- Username + display name
- Following / Followers / Likes counts (horizontal row)
- Bio (80 chars max -- extremely constrained)
- Single link URL
- "Follow" and social link buttons

**Primary CTA:** "Follow" button (red/pink)

**Trust Signals:**

- Verified badge
- Follower and Likes count
- Bio link (often to a link-in-bio page)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** None. Trust = audience size + video engagement.

**Visual Treatment:** Even more minimal than Instagram. The profile is just a header above a vertical-scroll video grid. Two tabs: Videos and Liked. The content grid IS the identity. Mobile-native, dark mode default. Personality expressed entirely through content, not profile decoration.

---

### Linktree / Link-in-Bio Pages

**Hero Zone (entire page is essentially "above the fold"):**

- Profile photo (circular, centered, top)
- Display name
- Short bio / tagline
- Vertical stack of link buttons (the core product)

**Primary CTA:** Each link button IS a CTA -- the whole page is CTAs

**Trust Signals:**

- Verified badge
- Social proof through recognized brand/link destinations
- Number/quality of links (curated vs. chaotic)
- Visual consistency with the person's brand

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** None. This is a routing page, not a profile.

**Visual Treatment:** Extremely minimal. Single column, centered. Customizable themes (colors, fonts, button styles). 5-7 links is the sweet spot. "Priority links" can be pinned to top. The page is a vertical menu dressed up with brand aesthetics. Background images/gradients, button animations (hover effects). Feels personal -- like a digital business card.

---

### About.me / Personal Portfolio Sites

**Hero Zone:**

- Full-bleed hero image or background (the entire viewport)
- Name (large, centered or left-aligned display type)
- One-line tagline / "I am a..."
- CTA button ("Hire Me", "Say Hello", "View Work")
- Sometimes: social media icon row

**Primary CTA:** Single, prominent button (varies: "Contact", "View Portfolio", "Download Resume")

**Trust Signals:**

- Visual quality of the hero image/design itself (the design IS the credential)
- Client logos row ("Worked with...")
- Awards / press mentions
- Social media follower counts (linked icons)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** Testimonial sections (if present) live below the fold, often as a carousel or quote blocks. They're present but not primary.

**Visual Treatment:** The most expressive category. Full-viewport hero sections, custom typography, animation, video backgrounds. Personality over information density. Generous whitespace. Often single-page scroll. The aesthetic choices communicate as much as the content -- a designer's site looks designed, a developer's might be minimal and typographic, a photographer's is image-dominant.

## 3. Community / Organization Profiles

### Facebook Groups

**Hero Zone:**

- Cover photo (1640x856px, full width)
- Group name (large, bold)
- Privacy label ("Public" or "Private")
- Member count + "X posts per day" activity metric
- Short description (1-2 lines visible)
- "Join Group" button
- Tab row: Discussion / Members / Events / Media / Files

**Primary CTA:** "Join Group" (blue button, prominent)

**Trust Signals:**

- Member count (the dominant metric)
- Activity frequency ("12 posts/day")
- "Friends who are members" (mutual social proof)
- Admin/moderator names and photos
- Group rules (visible in sidebar -- signals intentionality)
- Privacy setting (Private = exclusive, curated)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** No formal endorsements. Social proof = member count + activity level + friends already in the group.

**Visual Treatment:** Functional, platform-native. The group page is a feed with a header. Very little customization beyond the cover photo. Dense -- the feed starts almost immediately below the hero. Tabs provide structure. The "About" section and rules are accessible but not in-your-face.

---

### Discord Server Pages

**Hero Zone (Discovery/invite page):**

- Server icon (circular, left)
- Server name + verification badge
- Member count + online count
- Short description
- "Join" button
- Server banner image (if boosted)

**Primary CTA:** "Join Server" (green button)

**Trust Signals:**

- Verification badge (Discord-verified)
- Member count + online count (activity proof)
- Boost level (visual flair, more features)
- Server age
- "Partnered" status
- Well-organized channel structure (visible in preview)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** None. The channel list and member activity ARE the trust signals.

**Visual Treatment:** Compact, dark-themed. The server discovery page is minimal -- icon, name, description, join. Once inside, the server identity lives in the channel sidebar, roles, and custom emojis. The "profile" is really the server structure itself.

---

### Meetup Group Pages

**Hero Zone:**

- Group photo (large, event-style)
- Group name
- Location + member count
- Organizer name and photo
- Next event card (date, title, RSVP count)
- "Join this group" button

**Primary CTA:** "Join this group" (to RSVP for events)

**Trust Signals:**

- Member count
- Event history (past events with attendance numbers)
- Organizer reputation (number of events organized)
- Photo galleries from past events
- Next upcoming event (shows the group is active)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** Reviews of past events (attendee comments). These appear on individual event pages, not the group profile directly. Group "About" section includes organizer-written description.

**Visual Treatment:** Event-centric. The group profile exists to funnel people into event RSVPs. Calendar and event cards dominate below the fold. Photos from past events serve as social proof. Functional, not aesthetically ambitious.

---

### Nonprofit Pages (Charity Navigator / GuideStar)

**Hero Zone:**

- Organization logo + name
- Rating / score (Charity Navigator's star rating or score out of 100)
- Mission statement (1-2 sentences)
- Location + EIN number
- "Donate" button
- Financial health indicators (program expense ratio, etc.)

**Primary CTA:** "Donate" button

**Trust Signals:**

- Rating/score (the DOMINANT element -- large, colored, front-and-center)
- Financial transparency metrics (percentage of funds going to programs)
- IRS determination letter status
- Years of operation
- Board of directors listing
- Annual revenue / assets figures

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** No user reviews. Trust is built through financial data transparency, third-party ratings, and organizational governance disclosures. The rating IS the endorsement.

**Visual Treatment:** Institutional, data-dense. Tables, charts, financial metrics. White background, structured layout. Feels like a report more than a profile. Trust is built through transparency, not aesthetics.

## 4. Service Provider Profiles

### Thumbtack

**Hero Zone:**

- Business name + profile photo
- Star rating + number of reviews
- "X hires on Thumbtack" (social proof metric)
- Location + years in business
- Badges: "Great Value", "Top Pro", etc.
- Price estimate range
- "Check availability" or "Request quote" button

**Primary CTA:** "Request a Quote" / "Check Availability"

**Trust Signals:**

- Star rating (dominant, top of profile)
- Number of hires on platform
- "Top Pro" badge (algorithmically awarded)
- Response time ("Responds within X hours")
- Years in business
- License/insurance verification
- Background check badge

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Reviews are prominently placed, often the second section after the hero
- Star breakdown (5-star, 4-star histogram)
- Individual reviews with photos, project details, and dates
- Reviews mentionable specific services for relevance

**Visual Treatment:** Clean, white, conversion-optimized. The profile is a landing page for a specific service provider. Information hierarchy: identity -> trust score -> reviews -> portfolio -> about. Photos of completed work appear as a gallery. Mobile-optimized for on-the-go hiring.

### Angi (formerly Angie's List)

**Hero Zone:**

- Business name + logo
- Letter grade (A through F -- the iconic trust signal)
- Star rating + review count
- Service categories
- Location + service area
- "Request a Quote" button

**Primary CTA:** "Request a Quote"

**Trust Signals:**

- Letter grade (A-F, report-card style -- highly distinctive)
- Background check verification
- License verification
- Annual screening
- BBB rating cross-reference
- "Angi Certified" badge
- Response rate

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Reviews are central to the experience
- Letter grade is derived from reviews
- Reviews include specific project details (scope, cost, timeline)
- Photo verification of completed work

**Visual Treatment:** Trust-forward, institutional. The letter grade dominates. White/light background, structured sections. Feels like Consumer Reports for services. More formal than Thumbtack, more data-driven than Yelp.

### Yelp Business Profile

**Hero Zone:**

- Business name (large)
- Star rating (the iconic red stars) + review count
- Category tags ("Italian Restaurant", "$$")
- Open/closed status + hours
- Photo carousel (user-submitted + business photos)
- "Write a Review" + "Add Photo" buttons
- Address + phone + website link

**Primary CTA:** "Write a Review" (for existing customers) / "Get Directions" or "Call" (for prospective customers)

**Trust Signals:**

- Star rating (dominant, red, immediately visible)
- Review count (volume = legitimacy)
- Photo quantity and quality
- "Yelp Verified" badge
- Response from business owner (on reviews)
- Hours accuracy and claimed profile status
- Enhanced profiles increase leads by 38%

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Reviews ARE the product. They dominate the page below the hero
- "Recommended Reviews" filtered by Yelp's algorithm
- Photo reviews weighted more heavily
- Business owner responses visible inline
- Review highlights / excerpts pulled to the top

**Visual Treatment:** Red accents on white. Photo-forward (carousel is prominent). Dense review text below. The page is fundamentally a review aggregation page with business identity on top. Functional, high-information-density, optimized for decision-making.

### Google Business Profile

**Hero Zone (in Maps/Search panel):**

- Business name
- Star rating + review count
- Category
- Open/closed status
- Photo carousel (auto-selected by Google)
- Address + directions link
- Phone + website
- "Call" / "Directions" / "Website" action buttons

**Primary CTA:** "Call" or "Directions" (action-oriented, immediate)

**Trust Signals:**

- Star rating + review count (most trusted review signal on the internet)
- Google verification badge
- Photos (especially recent, user-submitted)
- Q&A section
- Business attributes (wheelchair accessible, outdoor seating, etc.)
- "Story-like" review format (2025 update -- swipeable reviews with photos)
- Review freshness matters (3+ months old posts de-prioritized)

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:**

- Reviews are immediately accessible, often auto-expanded
- "Most relevant" review shown first
- Review photos displayed prominently
- Business owner responses visible
- 300 reviews at 4.6 > 12 reviews at 5.0 (volume matters)

**Visual Treatment:** Constrained by Google's UI. White card in Maps sidebar or search panel. Compact, information-dense, standardized across all businesses. The design serves the decision: "Should I go here?" Photo carousel is the most visual element. Extremely utilitarian.

## 5. Creative Portfolios

**Hero Zone:**

- Project cover image (1400px wide, full-bleed)
- Project title
- Creator name + avatar
- Appreciation count + views + comments
- Tools used (tag pills)

**Primary CTA:** "Appreciate" (like) or "Save" (to collections)

**Trust Signals:**

- Appreciation/view counts
- Featured by Adobe curation
- Quality of the case study presentation itself
- Tools/software badges

**Endorsements/Reviews Position:** Comments section below the project. No formal endorsements -- peer engagement (appreciations, comments) is the social proof.

**Visual Treatment:** Full-width, image-dominant. Long-scroll case studies with inline text. The project page is a visual essay. White background, generous spacing. Projects are 1400px wide images stacked vertically. The presentation quality IS the portfolio -- a beautifully composed Behance project demonstrates skill through its own execution.

- The shot itself (image/animation, full-width, ~800px tall)
- Title + description below
- Creator avatar + name + "Follow" button

**Visual Treatment:** Single-image focused. The shot dominates -- everything else is secondary. Clean white background, minimal UI chrome. The platform gets out of the way of the work. Masonry grid on profile pages. Pink accent color. Feels like a curated gallery.

## Implications for Personus.ai

Given that Personus serves multiple persona types (person, organization, shadow) with different purposes (professional discovery, community membership, service offering), the design should be **adaptive by entity type and context** rather than following a single platform's pattern.

### Recommended Hero Zone Structure (top ~400px)

```
┌─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┐
│  ┌──────┐  Display Name (Fraunces, large)        [Contact] │
│  │      │  Headline / tagline                     [Share]   │
│  │ PHOTO│  Entity type badge (person/org/shadow)            │
│  │      │  Location · Joined · Last active                  │
│  └──────┘                                                   │
│                                                             │
│  ┌─────────┐ ┌─────────┐ ┌──────────┐ ┌──────────────────┐ │
│  │Qualities│ │Endorsed │ │Offerings │ │Completeness      │ │
│  │  count  │ │  count  │ │  count   │ │  ring (owner)    │ │
│  └─────────┘ └─────────┘ └──────────┘ └──────────────────┘ │
│                                                             │
│  Bio / About (2-3 lines, "see more" pattern)                │
│                                                             │
│  [skill tag] [skill tag] [skill tag] [skill tag] +N more    │
└─────────────────────────────────────────────────────────────┘
```

### Key Design Decisions Informed by This Research

1. **Endorsements should be MORE prominent than on LinkedIn.** Personus's core value prop is capability-based discovery with trust. Endorsement counts belong in the hero zone, not buried mid-page. Follow the Upwork model (Job Success Score in hero) rather than LinkedIn (endorsements buried in Skills section).

2. **The primary CTA should be "Contact" (mediated), not "Connect" (social).** This aligns with the service provider model (Thumbtack, Upwork) where the goal is action, not network accumulation.

3. **Skills/qualities should use the tag-pill pattern** (universal across LinkedIn, Upwork, Behance, Wellfound). This is the most understood UI pattern for capabilities.

4. **Organization personas should borrow from the community/group playbook:** member count, activity metrics, and "join" as the CTA -- similar to Facebook Groups and Discord servers.

5. **Shadow personas need the Linktree simplicity.** Since they're created for non-users, the page should be minimal: photo, name, capabilities, endorser attribution. A single CTA: "Claim this persona."

6. **Visual density should adapt by entity type:**
   - Person personas: Medium density (LinkedIn-like but more airy, following the Wellfound aesthetic)
   - Organization personas: Slightly denser (community metrics, member grid, similar to Meetup)
   - Shadow personas: Minimal (Linktree-level simplicity)

7. **"Completeness as motivation" should follow the gamification pattern** seen across platforms -- progress rings (LinkedIn), completion percentage (Upwork), but never punitive. Empty state should feel like opportunity, not failure.

8. **Offerings should be displayed as cards** (like Fiverr Gigs or Thumbtack service cards), not as text lists. Each offering gets a mini-card with title, brief description, and availability indicator.

9. **The dark-mode, card-based elevation design** already defined in the Personus design system aligns well with the Discord/modern-portfolio aesthetic and stands out from the white-background defaults of LinkedIn/Upwork/Fiverr.

10. **Review/endorsement display should show relationship context** (a pattern NO platform does well today). "Endorsed by Sarah Chen, who worked with Alex for 3 years at Acme Corp" is more powerful than a generic endorsement count. This is a differentiator.
